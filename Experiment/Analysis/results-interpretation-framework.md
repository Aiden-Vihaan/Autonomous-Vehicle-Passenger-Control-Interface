Results Interpretation Framework

Project: Autonomous Vehicle Passenger Control Interface
Experimental Version: EV-1.0
Purpose: Standardise interpretation of empirical findings

---

1. Interpretation Principle

The central analytical principle is:

«The data determine the conclusion; the hypothesis does not determine the data.»

The project investigates whether different forms of explanation influence passenger understanding, trust, and cognitive workload.

It does not assume that more explanation is necessarily better.

---

2. Hypothesis Interpretation

The final results will evaluate each hypothesis using one of four classifications:

Classification| Meaning
Supported| Observed evidence is consistent with the predicted direction and analysis
Partially supported| Evidence supports some but not all aspects of the prediction
Not supported| Evidence does not demonstrate the predicted effect
Inconclusive| Data quality, sample size, measurement, or design prevents a reliable conclusion

This prevents forced binary conclusions from ambiguous evidence.

---

3. Primary Interpretation

The primary question is:

«Does explanation condition affect passenger comprehension?»

The preferred interpretation sequence is:

1. inspect condition distributions;
2. compare descriptive performance;
3. evaluate uncertainty;
4. account for participant/trial structure;
5. perform inferential analysis where justified;
6. estimate effect magnitude;
7. interpret practical relevance.

---

4. Explanation Specificity

If contextual explanations outperform minimal explanations, the result may suggest that specificity contributes to passenger understanding.

However, the result would not establish that increasingly detailed explanations are always better.

If minimal explanations perform similarly to contextual explanations, this may indicate that concise explanations provide sufficient information while reducing interface complexity.

If contextual explanations perform worse, this may indicate an information-density or cognitive-processing cost.

---

5. Trust Interpretation

Trust is treated as a calibration-related construct.

A desirable pattern is not simply:

«higher trust.»

A more meaningful Human Factors interpretation is:

«appropriate trust associated with improved understanding of the vehicle's behaviour.»

If trust increases without comprehension improving, the result should be interpreted cautiously.

---

6. Workload Interpretation

Workload is interpreted alongside comprehension.

Example analytical matrix

Comprehension| Workload| Possible interpretation
↑| ↓| Strong candidate interaction improvement
↑| ≈| Understanding improves without measurable workload cost
↑| ↑| Understanding benefit with cognitive trade-off
≈| ↓| Lower demand without clear comprehension benefit
≈| ≈| Limited measurable effect
↓| ↑| Potentially problematic interaction
↓| ↓| Less processing but poorer understanding

The arrows represent possible result patterns, not observed findings.

---

7. Design Decision Logic

Empirical results will inform design decisions using the following logic:

Observed finding
      ↓
Human Factors interpretation
      ↓
Design implication
      ↓
Prototype modification
      ↓
Validation requirement

A design modification is therefore justified by evidence rather than aesthetic preference alone.

---

8. Evidence Hierarchy

Design conclusions are prioritised according to:

1. primary outcome evidence;
2. behavioural measures;
3. secondary quantitative measures;
4. qualitative observations;
5. theoretical interpretation;
6. speculative future opportunities.

The final report clearly distinguishes these levels.

---

9. Limitations

The final interpretation will consider:

- sample size;
- participant characteristics;
- simulator/prototype context;
- task realism;
- explanation wording;
- scenario selection;
- repeated exposure;
- measurement limitations;
- ecological validity;
- possible learning effects;
- possible order effects;
- technical issues;
- and protocol deviations.

---

10. Generalisability Boundary

The findings are interpreted as evidence concerning the tested interaction design.

They are not automatically generalised to:

- all autonomous vehicles;
- all passenger populations;
- all driving environments;
- real-world traffic;
- production autonomous-driving systems;
- or safety-critical deployment.

---

11. Final Results Writing Standard

Each empirical result in the final report should follow this structure:

«Finding → Evidence → Interpretation → Design implication → Limitation»

For example, once actual data exist:

«Finding: Participants demonstrated higher comprehension under [condition].
Evidence: The observed condition-level difference was [dataset-derived result].
Interpretation: This pattern suggests [careful interpretation].
Design implication: The interface should therefore [evidence-linked change].
Limitation: The result should be interpreted within [specific limitation].»

No numerical value is inserted until it exists in the dataset.

---

12. Research Integrity

The project explicitly rejects:

- fabricated participants;
- simulated participant responses presented as real;
- invented statistical significance;
- invented effect sizes;
- invented qualitative quotes;
- invented usability observations;
- selective deletion of inconvenient results.

This standard applies to:

- GitHub;
- portfolio case study;
- final report;
- presentation;
- CV;
- and application materials.

---

13. Final Analytical Goal

The ultimate objective is not to demonstrate that the proposed interface was successful.

The objective is to determine:

«Which explanation strategy, under which interaction conditions, appears to support passenger understanding while maintaining appropriate trust and manageable cognitive workload?»

That distinction is central to the Human Factors contribution of the project.
