---

1. Purpose

This analysis plan defines how the collected experimental data will be examined.

The analysis strategy is established before formal results are interpreted to reduce the risk of changing analytical decisions in response to observed outcomes.

The analysis will distinguish:

1. descriptive results;
2. inferential results where the sample and design justify them;
3. qualitative observations;
4. protocol deviations;
5. limitations.

---

2. Primary Analytical Question

«Does explanation condition affect passenger understanding of unexpected autonomous-vehicle behaviour?»

The primary outcome is comprehension.

---

3. Secondary Analytical Questions

The analysis will additionally examine whether explanation condition is associated with differences in:

- response time;
- trust;
- perceived understanding;
- perceived workload;
- task success;
- interaction errors.

---

4. Analysis Variables

Independent Variable

Explanation condition

A = No explanation
B = Minimal explanation
C = Contextual explanation

---

Primary Dependent Variable

Comprehension

Measured using the predefined comprehension scoring procedure.

---

Secondary Dependent Variables

Response time

Continuous measurement where available.

Trust

Subjective participant rating.

Workload

NASA-TLX-based measurement.

Perceived understanding

Subjective understanding rating.

Task success

Successful completion of the required interaction.

Interaction errors

Predefined observable errors.

---

5. Data Cleaning

Before analysis, the dataset will be checked for:

- duplicate records;
- impossible values;
- missing values;
- inconsistent condition labels;
- incorrect scenario identifiers;
- technical failures;
- protocol deviations.

Data cleaning will not alter participant responses simply because they appear unusual.

---

6. Missing Data

Missing data will be explicitly identified.

The project will not substitute invented values for missing observations.

Depending on the amount and pattern of missingness, analyses will either:

- use available observations for the relevant measure; or
- report that the measure could not be analysed reliably.

The final report will state the actual missing-data situation.

---

7. Descriptive Analysis

For each explanation condition, the following will be summarized where data permit:

Comprehension

- number of observations;
- correct-response proportion;
- mean/median score as appropriate.

Response time

- mean;
- median;
- dispersion;
- range where informative.

Trust

- central tendency;
- dispersion;
- distribution.

Workload

- central tendency;
- dispersion;
- distribution.

Perceived understanding

- central tendency;
- dispersion;
- distribution.

Descriptive statistics will be presented before inferential interpretation.

---

8. Primary Comparison

The principal comparison is:

No explanation
      vs.
Minimal explanation
      vs.
Contextual explanation

The analysis will determine whether observed differences in comprehension are consistent with an explanation-condition effect.

The exact statistical test will depend on the final study design, sample size, measurement scale, and distribution of the collected data.

No statistical test will be selected merely because it produces a desired result.

---

9. Repeated-Measures Consideration

If participants experience more than one explanation condition, participant-level dependence will be considered during analysis.

Observations from the same participant will not automatically be treated as independent observations.

The final statistical model will therefore follow the actual experimental structure.

---

10. Effect Size

Where inferential testing is appropriate, effect sizes will be reported alongside significance testing.

The project will prioritize:

«magnitude and practical interpretation over p-values alone.»

An observed difference will not automatically be described as meaningful simply because it reaches a statistical threshold.

---

11. Trust Analysis

Trust scores will be compared across explanation conditions.

The interpretation will remain cautious.

For example:

«A higher reported trust score indicates greater reported trust in the presented interface/system.»

It does not establish:

«The autonomous vehicle is objectively more trustworthy.»

The experiment evaluates passenger perception rather than actual autonomous-system reliability.

---

12. Workload Analysis

Workload will be examined to identify whether additional explanation information appears associated with:

- reduced perceived effort;
- increased perceived effort;
- no meaningful change.

A useful conceptual interpretation is:

More information
       ↓
Potentially better understanding
       +
Potentially greater cognitive demand

Therefore, increased explanation detail will not automatically be interpreted as better design.

---

13. Understanding–Trust Relationship

Where the dataset is sufficient, the analysis may explore whether comprehension and trust move together.

The conceptual question is:

«Do passengers who understand the autonomous event better also report more appropriately calibrated trust?»

This is exploratory unless the final study design explicitly supports a stronger hypothesis test.

---

14. Response-Time Interpretation

Response time will not be interpreted independently of accuracy.

For example:

Fast + incorrect

does not necessarily represent better interaction than:

Slower + correct

Therefore response time will be interpreted alongside comprehension.

---

15. Qualitative Analysis

Participant comments and experimenter observations will be treated separately from quantitative measurements.

Potential recurring themes include:

- clarity;
- reassurance;
- information overload;
- perceived transparency;
- uncertainty;
- distraction;
- trust;
- explanation preference;
- wording concerns.

Themes will only be reported when supported by actual collected comments.

No qualitative theme will be presented as a finding before evidence is available.

---

16. Protocol-Deviation Analysis

All deviations will be reviewed before final analysis.

For each deviation:

1. identify what happened;
2. identify which participant/trial was affected;
3. determine which measurement may have been affected;
4. determine whether the observation remains usable;
5. document the decision.

This prevents silent exclusion of inconvenient observations.

---

17. Pilot vs. Formal Data

Pilot observations and formal experimental observations are analytically separate.

Pilot

Purpose:

- detect technical problems;
- identify procedural ambiguity;
- assess prototype readiness;
- refine the experimental process.

Formal study

Purpose:

- generate empirical evidence for the research questions.

Pilot observations will therefore not be pooled with formal participant results unless the study design explicitly establishes a justified reason to do so.

---

18. Evidence Hierarchy

The final interpretation will follow this hierarchy:

Collected quantitative data
          ↓
Descriptive statistics
          ↓
Inferential analysis where justified
          ↓
Qualitative participant evidence
          ↓
Protocol observations
          ↓
Design interpretation

Claims will not exceed the strength of the evidence.

---

19. Interpretation Rules

The final report will distinguish between:

Evidence

«Participants in Condition C had a higher mean comprehension score than participants in Condition A.»

Interpretation

«The contextual explanation may have supported better understanding.»

Hypothesis-level conclusion

«The observed pattern is consistent with the hypothesis that contextual explanations improve comprehension.»

The project will not claim causality beyond what the actual experimental design supports.

---

20. Negative or Null Results

Null results will be treated as legitimate findings.

For example, if explanation conditions do not differ meaningfully in comprehension, the project will not reinterpret the result as a design success merely because explanations were theoretically expected to help.

A strong research project reports what happened rather than forcing the result to match the hypothesis.

---

21. Expected Figures

The final analysis may include:

1. comprehension by explanation condition;
2. response time by explanation condition;
3. trust by explanation condition;
4. workload by explanation condition;
5. perceived understanding by explanation condition;
6. condition-level error/task-success summary.

Figures will be generated from actual collected data only.

---

22. No Fabrication Rule

The following are explicitly prohibited:

- invented participant counts;
- invented means;
- invented p-values;
- invented effect sizes;
- invented qualitative quotations;
- invented usability problems;
- invented statistical significance;
- invented pilot observations.

If a measure was not collected, it will be marked as not collected.

If a result is inconclusive, it will be reported as inconclusive.

---

23. Limitations to Evaluate

The final discussion will consider limitations including, where applicable:

- prototype fidelity;
- sample size;
- participant demographics;
- ecological validity;
- simulated rather than real vehicle behaviour;
- familiarity with autonomous vehicles;
- learning effects;
- order effects;
- self-report limitations;
- workload measurement limitations;
- trust measurement limitations;
- absence of physiological or eye-tracking instrumentation;
- limited scenario diversity.

These limitations will be based on the actual study rather than inserted merely for completeness.

25. Decision Rule

The final conclusion will answer the research question using the evidence actually collected.

The project will classify conclusions as:

Supported

Evidence is sufficiently consistent with the proposed relationship.

Partially supported

Some outcomes support the proposition while others do not.

Not supported

The collected evidence does not support the proposed relationship.

Inconclusive

The available evidence is insufficient to make a reliable determination.

---

26. Final Research Position

The objective of this project is not to prove that explanations are universally beneficial.

The objective is to investigate how explanation design may influence passenger interaction with autonomous-vehicle behaviour.

The strongest outcome is therefore an evidence-based account of:

- what explanation information was presented;
- how passengers responded;
- what changed across conditions;
- what did not change;
- what the results mean for passenger-facing HMI design;
- and what remains unresolved.
