Pilot Observation Log

Project: Autonomous Vehicle Passenger Control Interface
Research Phase: Pilot Execution
Day: 27
Document Type: Structured Pilot Observation Record

---

1. Purpose

This document provides the standardized structure for recording observations during the pilot session.

Pilot observations are used to identify problems in the prototype, experimental procedure, measurement process, and participant interaction.

They are not treated as formal experimental findings.

---

2. Evidence Classification

Each observation is classified as one of the following:

Classification| Meaning
Observation| Something directly observed during the session
Technical Issue| A problem with the prototype or technical execution
Procedural Issue| A problem with the experimental procedure
Accessibility Issue| A barrier affecting interaction or comprehension
Protocol Deviation| A departure from the predefined procedure
Participant Feedback| A participant's explicit comment
Design Opportunity| A possible improvement that does not necessarily represent a problem

Interpretations and hypotheses are not recorded as observations.

---

3. Session Configuration

Study Configuration

Variable| Recorded Value
Prototype| High-fidelity experimental prototype
Research focus| Autonomous-vehicle explanations
Conditions| No explanation / Minimal / Contextual
Primary outcomes| Comprehension / Trust / Workload
Secondary outcomes| Situation awareness / Interaction performance / Response time / Transparency / Usability
Pilot purpose| Technical, procedural and methodological validation

The pilot is not included in formal hypothesis-testing analysis.

---

4. Briefing Observation

The participant briefing is evaluated for clarity.

The experimenter observes whether the participant can understand:

- their role as an autonomous-vehicle passenger;
- the expected interaction;
- the purpose of observing vehicle behaviour;
- the procedure for answering questions;
- the rating procedure;
- the workload assessment.

The experimenter avoids supplying information that could reveal the experimental hypothesis.

Observation record:
The briefing procedure is standardized and designed to minimize experimenter influence. Any participant request for clarification should be recorded as an observation rather than retrospectively interpreted.

---

5. Practice Observation

The practice interaction is evaluated for:

- navigation familiarity;
- understanding of the interaction sequence;
- comprehension-question familiarity;
- rating-scale familiarity;
- workload-assessment familiarity.

Practice responses are excluded from formal experimental results.

Observation record:
The practice stage is treated as a procedural familiarization stage. Any intervention required during practice should be documented separately from experimental-trial observations.

---

6. Experimental Trial Observation

For each scenario, the experimenter evaluates the following.

Event Recognition

- Did the participant notice the unexpected event?
- Did the participant notice the autonomous response?
- Was the system state understandable?

Explanation Interpretation

- Was the explanation noticed?
- Was the wording understandable?
- Did the participant appear confused by the explanation?
- Did the explanation appear visually distinguishable from other interface information?

Interaction

- Was navigation straightforward?
- Did the participant require assistance?
- Were any unexpected interactions observed?

Measurement

- Were comprehension questions understood?
- Were trust/experience ratings understandable?
- Was the workload procedure clear?

---

7. Condition-Specific Observation Framework

Condition A — No Explanation

The pilot checks whether the absence of explanation creates any unintended ambiguity in the interface.

The observation focuses on whether the autonomous response is sufficiently distinguishable from normal journey behaviour for the participant to recognize that an unexpected event has occurred.

No conclusion about trust or comprehension should be drawn from a single pilot interaction.

---

Condition B — Minimal Explanation

The pilot checks whether the short explanation communicates the immediate reason for the vehicle's response without requiring additional interpretation.

The observation focuses on:

- readability;
- brevity;
- event identification;
- semantic clarity;
- relationship between explanation and vehicle response.

---

Condition C — Contextual Explanation

The pilot checks whether the additional contextual information is understandable without creating unnecessary cognitive or visual complexity.

The observation focuses on:

- contextual clarity;
- information density;
- reading burden;
- connection between context and autonomous response;
- distinction between useful explanation and unnecessary detail.

---

8. Cross-Condition Integrity

The following must remain controlled:

- event;
- vehicle response;
- scenario;
- task;
- measurement procedure.

The explanation content is the intended difference.

If an unintended difference is discovered, it must be recorded as a methodological issue.

---

9. Accessibility Observation

The pilot checks:

Visual

- text readability;
- hierarchy;
- spacing;
- status visibility;
- warning visibility.

Interaction

- touch target accessibility;
- navigation clarity;
- interaction consistency.

Cognitive

- clarity of terminology;
- information density;
- distinguishability of system states;
- clarity of explanation.

Communication

- whether critical information depends on colour;
- whether icons are understandable;
- whether system status and explanation are distinguishable.

---

10. Technical Observation

The following technical events are monitored:

- prototype loading failure;
- missing screen;
- incorrect transition;
- broken interaction;
- incorrect scenario state;
- incorrect explanation;
- accidental duplicate information;
- unexpected interface behaviour.

Each technical issue is assigned a severity level.

---

11. Measurement Observation

The pilot evaluates whether the measurement process itself introduces avoidable problems.

Comprehension

Check whether questions are understandable and answerable from the preceding interaction.

Trust

Check whether the rating scale and wording are clear and neutral.

Workload

Check whether the NASA-TLX procedure can be completed without procedural confusion or excessive interruption.

---

12. Experimenter Intervention

Any intervention is recorded using the following principle:

«The experimenter should provide the minimum assistance necessary to resolve a technical or procedural problem and should avoid influencing participant interpretation.»

Intervention is treated as a potential protocol deviation when it occurs during an experimental trial.

---

15. Pilot Review

Following the session, the experimenter reviews:

Prototype

- Did the prototype operate as intended?
- Were any screens missing?
- Were state transitions correct?

Experimental Manipulation

- Were explanation conditions presented correctly?
- Was the manipulation isolated?

Participant Procedure

- Were instructions sufficient?
- Was experimenter intervention necessary?

Measurement

- Were comprehension questions understandable?
- Were trust ratings understandable?
- Was workload assessment practical?

Accessibility

- Were any accessibility barriers observed?

Data Integrity

- Were all required variables recordable?
- Were any responses lost or ambiguous?

---

16. Decision Framework

The pilot outcome is categorized according to evidence.

Proceed

The procedure is stable and no validity-threatening problem is identified.

Proceed With Minor Revision

Minor presentation or usability improvements are identified without changing the experimental manipulation.

Revise and Re-Pilot

A methodological, technical, accessibility, or measurement issue could affect formal results.

Redesign

The experimental architecture itself requires substantial modification.

---

17. Interpretation Boundary

The pilot does not support conclusions about:

- which explanation condition produces greater trust;
- which condition produces lower workload;
- which condition produces better comprehension;
- whether explanations improve situation awareness;
- statistical differences between conditions.

Those questions require appropriately collected formal experimental data.

---

18. Final Pilot Record

The final pilot report must contain:

1. prototype version;
2. session configuration;
3. procedure followed;
4. observations;
5. participant feedback where applicable;
6. technical issues;
7. accessibility issues;
8. protocol deviations;
9. corrective actions;
10. re-pilot decision;
11. final prototype version.

This creates a traceable link between pilot evidence and subsequent prototype changes.
