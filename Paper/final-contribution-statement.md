Final Project Contribution Statement

Autonomous Vehicle Passenger Control Interface

Research Focus

Explainable autonomous-vehicle behaviour and passenger understanding

---

1. Core Contribution

This project investigates how passenger-facing explanations can communicate unexpected autonomous-vehicle behaviour in a manner that supports understanding without introducing unnecessary cognitive burden.

The project combines Human Factors theory, explainable AI, UX design, accessibility, and experimental methodology into a single passenger-interface framework.

---

2. Problem

Autonomous vehicles can perform behaviours that passengers cannot directly interpret from the vehicle's physical actions alone.

A passenger may observe:

- unexpected braking;
- route changes;
- waiting;
- yielding;
- lane changes;
- or other autonomous responses.

Without appropriate communication, the passenger may be uncertain about what the system perceived, why it acted, and what will happen next.

---

3. Research Response

The project converts this problem into an experimentally structured question:

«How does the presentation of explanations for unexpected autonomous-vehicle behaviour affect passenger understanding, trust, and cognitive workload?»

Three explanation conditions are defined:

1. no explanation;
2. minimal explanation;
3. contextual explanation.

---

4. Human Factors Framework

The project evaluates explanation design through:

- comprehension;
- trust;
- cognitive workload;
- perceived understanding;
- response time;
- task success;
- interaction errors.

This prevents the evaluation from reducing explainability to a single subjective satisfaction measure.

---

5. Design Framework

The passenger explanation follows:

«What happened → What the vehicle is doing → What it means for the passenger»

Additional information is progressively disclosed rather than continuously displayed.

---

6. Central Design Principle

«Transparency should be useful, not maximal.»

The goal is not to expose every internal system decision.

The goal is to communicate the information passengers need to construct an appropriate understanding of the vehicle's behaviour.

---

---

8. Integrity Statement

The project distinguishes between methodological completion and empirical validation.

Where participant-level observations are not available, the project does not invent numerical results or causal conclusions.

All empirical claims must therefore be supported by recorded observations and reproducible analysis.

This approach ensures that the final artifact remains scientifically defensible.

---

9. Final Positioning

Autonomous Vehicle Passenger Control Interface is presented as a research-grade Human Factors/HCI project demonstrating how explainability can be designed, operationalised, and evaluated within an autonomous-vehicle passenger interface.

The project is a portfolio-scale research artifact and research-paper-style study framework rather than a claim of production vehicle validation or formal safety certification.

---

10. One-Sentence Contribution

«This project develops a Human Factors framework for designing and experimentally evaluating passenger-facing explanations of unexpected autonomous-vehicle behaviour, balancing understanding, calibrated trust, cognitive workload, accessibility, and safety.»
