Research Gap and Literature-to-Research Matrix

1. Purpose

The purpose of this document is to establish traceability between the reviewed literature, theoretical constructs, research questions, experimental variables, and design decisions.

The project does not treat individual studies as proof of a universal design rule.

Instead, the literature is used to establish:

- theoretical foundations;
- previously investigated variables;
- methodological precedents;
- unresolved questions;
- the rationale for the present research design.

4. Research Gap

4.1 What Existing Research Establishes

Previous research provides evidence that:

- explanations can affect trust-related responses;
- explanation format matters;
- explanation timing can influence interaction;
- perceived explainability is related to trust and acceptance;
- multimodal communication can influence situation awareness and trust;
- passenger activity can alter the usefulness of communication modalities;
- cognitive workload is relevant to multimodal and explanatory interfaces.

These findings establish that explanations matter.

---

4.2 What Remains Open

The literature does not establish a universally optimal passenger explanation strategy.

Several questions remain relevant:

Question 1 — How much explanation is appropriate?

A minimal explanation may be easier to process.

A contextual explanation may provide better understanding.

The relationship between additional specificity and cognitive demand therefore requires empirical examination.

Question 2 — Does better understanding necessarily produce higher trust?

Trust and understanding are related but conceptually distinct constructs.

A passenger may report confidence without accurately understanding the vehicle's behaviour.

The present project therefore measures objective comprehension separately from perceived understanding and trust-related response.

Question 3 — When should explanations appear?

Explanation timing can influence expectations and interpretation.

The interface therefore treats timing as a separate design dimension.

Question 4 — Does passenger context matter?

Passengers are not necessarily monitoring the vehicle continuously.

Their activity may influence whether visual, auditory, or multimodal explanations are effective.

Question 5 — How should explanation interact with safety?

An explanation must communicate system behaviour without competing with safety-critical controls.

---

5. Specific Research Gap

The specific research gap addressed by this project is:

«A need for a structured Human Factors approach that connects explanation specificity and timing with passenger comprehension while simultaneously considering trust, cognitive workload, passenger context, accessibility, and safety-oriented interface hierarchy.»

The project therefore moves beyond asking whether autonomous vehicles should explain themselves.

It investigates how explanation presentation can be structured and evaluated as a passenger-facing Human Factors problem.

The project adds a structured bridge between:

Literature

→ Human Factors theory

→ Experimental variables

→ Passenger HMI design

→ Measurement

→ Evidence

→ Design implications

This is an important methodological contribution because the project does not stop at creating an interface.

It establishes a traceable research workflow in which design decisions can be connected to theoretical constructs and evaluated through measurable passenger outcomes.

---

11. Boundaries of the Contribution

The project does not claim:

- that explanations always increase trust;
- that more detailed explanations are always better;
- that contextual explanations are universally superior;
- that visual explanations are superior to auditory explanations;
- that multimodal explanations are universally optimal;
- that the prototype represents production autonomous-vehicle behaviour;
- that the interface provides functional-safety certification;
- that the study validates an autonomous-driving system.

The contribution is instead methodological and design-oriented:

«a structured Human Factors framework for designing and evaluating passenger-facing explanations of unexpected autonomous-vehicle behaviour.»

---

12. Final Literature Review Conclusion

The literature indicates that autonomous-vehicle explanation is not a single-variable interface problem.

Explanation design interacts with:

- trust;
- understanding;
- situation awareness;
- cognitive workload;
- timing;
- risk;
- passenger activity;
- modality;
- accessibility.

The present project uses this intersection as its research foundation.
