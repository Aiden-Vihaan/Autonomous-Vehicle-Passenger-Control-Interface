Evidence Synthesis

Purpose

This document synthesizes findings across the academic literature reviewed for the Autonomous Vehicle Passenger Control Interface project.

The purpose of cross-source synthesis is to identify:

- Recurring findings.
- Differences between studies.
- Contradictory evidence.
- Important variables.
- Methodological limitations.
- Contextual limitations.
- Potential implications for the current research design.

This document does not establish a final research gap. It records the current state of evidence and will be updated as additional literature is reviewed.

---

Research Questions

Primary Research Question

«How does explainable AI feedback influence passenger understanding, trust, perceived workload, and interaction performance during unexpected events in a Level 4 autonomous vehicle?»

Secondary Research Questions

1. Does contextual explanation improve passenger understanding compared with minimal or no explanation?
2. Can explanations improve trust without creating excessive cognitive workload?
3. How does explanation timing and presentation affect interpretation of unexpected events?
4. Can multimodal and accessible explanations improve recognition of safety-critical system states?

---

Synthesis Approach

The literature will not be treated as a collection of independent summaries.

Instead, findings will be compared across:

- Research context.
- Participant population.
- Automation level.
- Task type.
- Explanation type.
- Explanation timing.
- Information presentation.
- Outcome measures.
- Experimental methodology.

This is important because a finding observed in one context may not necessarily generalize to passenger-facing Level 4 autonomous vehicles.

Potential Confounding Variables

The following factors may influence outcomes independently of explanation design:

- Prior experience with autonomous vehicles.
- General technology familiarity.
- Familiarity with AI systems.
- Baseline trust in automation.
- Scenario severity.
- Event type.
- Interface familiarity.
- Individual differences in cognitive ability or risk perception.
- Explanation reading time.
- Passenger attention.

These variables should be considered during experimental design.

---

Methodological Observations

Across the literature, attention should be given to differences in:

- Laboratory versus real-world environments.
- Simulated versus physical vehicles.
- Driver versus passenger populations.
- Normal versus unexpected scenarios.
- Short-term versus repeated interaction.
- Subjective versus behavioural measures.
- Within-subject versus between-subject designs.

These differences may explain apparently contradictory findings.

---

Evidence Confidence

Evidence will be classified according to:

High Confidence

Multiple methodologically appropriate studies produce broadly consistent findings.

Moderate Confidence

Evidence exists but is limited by context, methodology, sample, or inconsistent findings.

Low Confidence

Evidence is sparse, indirect, or highly context-dependent.

Unknown

Insufficient literature has been reviewed to make a meaningful assessment.

Research Integrity

The synthesis will:

- Distinguish evidence from interpretation.
- Preserve contradictory findings.
- Avoid treating all studies as equally strong.
- Avoid generalizing driver findings directly to passengers.
- Avoid assuming that increased trust is always desirable.
- Avoid assuming that more explanation is always better.
- Avoid claiming a research gap before sufficient literature has been reviewed.
- Avoid claiming causal relationships where the evidence is correlational.
