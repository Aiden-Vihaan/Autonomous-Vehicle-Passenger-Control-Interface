Design Implications

Principle 1 — Explain Unexpected Behaviour

Explanations should be triggered by passenger-relevant events.

Principle 2 — Lead With the Passenger's Question

The first information should answer:

«“Why did the vehicle do that?”»

Principle 3 — Use Progressive Disclosure

Immediate:
CAUSE + ACTION + IMPACT

Optional:
ADDITIONAL CONTEXT

Principle 4 — Keep Safety Outside the Explanation Hierarchy

Safety controls remain persistent and independent.

Principle 5 — Couple Explanation to the Event

Explanations should appear sufficiently close to the event to preserve temporal association.

Principle 6 — Preserve Semantic Equivalence

Visual and auditory versions should communicate equivalent essential information.

Principle 7 — Do Not Simulate Certainty

The interface should distinguish:

known
estimated
uncertain
unavailable

Principle 8 — Explain Passenger Impact

System-oriented:

«“Pedestrian detected.”»

Passenger-oriented:

«“Pedestrian entering crossing — slowing to maintain distance.”»

Principle 9 — Never Present Stale State as Live

System status must communicate degradation honestly.

Principle 10 — Evaluate Understanding Directly

Objective comprehension should complement subjective ratings.
