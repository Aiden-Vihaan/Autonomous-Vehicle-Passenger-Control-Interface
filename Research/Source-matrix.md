Purpose

This document provides a structured matrix for evaluating academic sources relevant to the research project.

The purpose is not to collect references for the sake of increasing the bibliography. Each source should contribute evidence that helps answer the research questions, establish the theoretical foundation, identify limitations in existing work, or inform a design or evaluation decision.

---

Research Questions

Primary Research Question

«How does explainable AI feedback influence passenger understanding, trust, perceived workload, and interaction performance during unexpected events in a Level 4 autonomous vehicle?»

Secondary Research Questions

1. Does contextual explanation improve passenger understanding compared with minimal or no explanation?
2. Can explanations improve trust without creating excessive cognitive workload?
3. How does explanation timing and presentation affect interpretation of unexpected events?
4. Can multimodal and accessible explanations improve recognition of safety-critical system states?

Evidence Strength

Sources should be classified conservatively.

Strong

Use when the study has a strong methodology, clearly reported results, appropriate participants/context, and findings directly relevant to the research question.

Moderate

Use when the source provides useful empirical or theoretical evidence but has limitations in sample, context, methodology, or direct applicability.

Limited

Use when the source is useful for background or hypothesis generation but provides weak or indirect evidence.

Not Yet Evaluated

Use temporarily when the source has been identified but has not yet been critically reviewed.

---

Source Selection Principles

A source should be retained when it contributes meaningfully to at least one of the following:

- Understanding passenger interaction with autonomous vehicles.
- Understanding explainable AI or explanation design.
- Understanding trust in automation.
- Understanding situation awareness.
- Understanding cognitive workload.
- Understanding human-autonomy interaction.
- Supporting safety-critical interface design.
- Supporting the methodology or measurement strategy.
- Identifying limitations or contradictory findings.
- Informing a specific design decision.

A source should not be included solely because it contains keywords related to autonomous vehicles or artificial intelligence.

---

Contradictory Evidence

Contradictory findings should be recorded rather than excluded.

For each important disagreement between studies, document:

- What the studies investigated.
- How their methods differed.
- Whether participant populations differed.
- Whether the task or automation context differed.
- Whether explanation type or timing differed.
- Whether the outcome measure differed.
- Which interpretation is better supported.
- Whether the contradiction creates a research opportunity.

---

Research Gap Assessment

No research gap should be declared until sufficient literature has been reviewed.

Potential gap categories to investigate include:

- Passenger-facing rather than driver-facing autonomous vehicle interfaces.
- Explanation of unexpected autonomous vehicle behaviour.
- Explanation timing during safety-relevant events.
- Relationship between explanation and passenger understanding.
- Relationship between explanation and calibrated trust.
- Relationship between explanation and cognitive workload.
- Accessibility of explanations during safety-critical events.
- Interaction performance under unexpected autonomous-system behaviour.

These are investigation areas, not confirmed research gaps.
