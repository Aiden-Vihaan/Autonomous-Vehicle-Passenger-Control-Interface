Project Charter

1. Project Title

Autonomous Vehicle Passenger Control Interface

2. Project Type

This project is an internship-based product design and Human–Machine Interaction project focused on the passenger experience of Level 4 and Level 5 autonomous vehicles.

The project combines product requirements, UX design, interaction design, explainable AI communication, accessibility, prototyping, and Human Factors evaluation.

The existing product scope and terminology are retained throughout the project.

---

3. Project Context

Autonomous vehicles fundamentally change the relationship between the human and the vehicle.

In a conventional vehicle, the driver continuously observes the environment, interprets vehicle behaviour, and retains direct control through steering, braking, and acceleration.

In a highly automated vehicle, the passenger does not perform these driving functions. Instead, the passenger must interpret and rely on an autonomous driving system whose decisions may not always be immediately understandable.

This creates an important Human–Machine Interaction problem.

A passenger may observe the vehicle slowing down, stopping, changing lanes, rerouting, or entering a degraded operational state without understanding why the vehicle has taken that action.

The passenger-facing interface therefore has an important role beyond displaying information. It must support understanding, appropriate trust, situation awareness, and safe interaction without unnecessarily increasing cognitive workload.

---

4. Problem Statement

Passengers in highly automated vehicles may experience uncertainty when the autonomous system performs unexpected actions or encounters unusual situations.

Examples include:

- unexpected braking;
- pedestrian or cyclist detection;
- emergency vehicles;
- road construction;
- unusual road users;
- route changes;
- degraded sensor conditions;
- connectivity degradation;
- emergency or minimal-risk situations.

The central problem addressed by this project is:

How can a passenger-facing autonomous vehicle interface communicate system perception, intent, and state in a manner that improves passenger understanding and supports appropriately calibrated trust without creating unnecessary cognitive workload?

---

5. Existing Product Objective

The interface is designed to provide passengers with a calm, understandable, accessible, and controllable in-cabin experience during autonomous travel.

The existing product principles remain the foundation of the design:

1. Trust is the product.
2. Show the “why”, not just the “what”.
3. Calm by default, present in a crisis.
4. One tap to safety.
5. Accessible to everyone.

These principles will not be replaced.

Instead, the Human Factors component of the project will investigate whether specific interface decisions actually support the intended passenger outcomes.

---

6. Human Factors Objective

The Human Factors objective is to investigate how different forms of autonomous-system explanation influence:

- passenger comprehension;
- understanding of autonomous vehicle behaviour;
- trust in the autonomous system;
- appropriate reliance on the system;
- interaction performance;
- perceived workload;
- ability to identify important system states.

The project therefore moves beyond asking:

“Does this interface look good?”

and investigates:

“Does this interface help people correctly understand and respond to autonomous-system behaviour?”

---

7. Research Direction

The project will treat explainable AI communication as a Human–Machine Interaction mechanism rather than only a visual design feature.

The proposed conceptual relationship is:

Autonomous-system behaviour

↓

System explanation

↓

Passenger understanding

↓

Trust calibration

↓

Appropriate passenger response

The interface should provide sufficient information to explain important autonomous-system decisions without overwhelming the passenger with unnecessary technical information.

---

8. Primary Research Question

How does explainable AI feedback influence passenger understanding, trust, perceived workload, and interaction performance during unexpected events in a Level 4 autonomous vehicle?

---

9. Secondary Research Questions

RQ2

Does contextual explanation improve passengers’ understanding of autonomous vehicle behaviour compared with minimal or absent explanation?

RQ3

Can explanations improve passenger trust without producing excessive cognitive workload?

RQ4

How does explanation timing and presentation affect passenger ability to correctly interpret unexpected autonomous-system events?

RQ5

Can multimodal and accessible explanations improve recognition of safety-critical system states?

---

10. Research Hypothesis Direction

The hypotheses will be formally specified after the literature review and experimental design.

The initial hypothesis direction is:

H1 — Comprehension

Passengers receiving contextual explanations will demonstrate greater understanding of autonomous-system behaviour than passengers receiving no or minimal explanation.

H2 — Interaction Performance

Passengers receiving contextual explanations will make fewer interpretation or interaction errors during unexpected events.

H3 — Trust Calibration

Appropriately designed explanations will improve trust calibration rather than simply increasing overall trust.

H4 — Workload

Explanations that provide relevant contextual information without unnecessary detail will improve understanding without producing a substantial increase in perceived workload.

These hypotheses are provisional and will be refined after reviewing the relevant Human Factors and Human–Machine Interaction literature.

---

11. Proposed Experimental Concept

The project will investigate multiple explanation conditions.

Condition A — No Explanation

The vehicle performs an unexpected action with minimal passenger-facing explanation.

Example:

Vehicle slowing

---

Condition B — Basic Explanation

The interface provides a concise explanation.

Example:

Slowing for pedestrian

---

Condition C — Contextual Explanation

The interface provides a concise explanation containing the relevant context and intended action.

Example:

Pedestrian entering the crossing ahead. Slowing to maintain a safe distance.

The final wording will be developed through the design process and evaluated experimentally.

---

12. Proposed Evaluation Measures

The final measures will be determined after the literature review and experimental design.

Potential measures include:

Objective measures

- comprehension accuracy;
- response time;
- task completion;
- interaction errors;
- correct identification of system state;
- appropriate response to safety-related events.

Subjective measures

- perceived workload;
- perceived understanding;
- trust in automation;
- perceived transparency;
- usability;
- confidence in interpreting system behaviour.

Validated instruments will be selected where appropriate rather than creating unnecessary custom scales.

---

13. Human Factors Framework

The project will investigate the applicability of established Human Factors concepts to the interface design.

Potential frameworks include:

Trust in Automation

Used to understand whether interface explanations support appropriately calibrated reliance rather than blind trust.

Situation Awareness

Used to examine whether the interface helps passengers understand what is happening, why it is happening, and what may happen next.

Rasmussen's Skills–Rules–Knowledge framework

Used to consider different levels of human cognitive processing during routine, unusual, and unexpected interaction situations.

SEEV

Used to reason about visual attention and the placement/salience of important information in the passenger interface.

Hick–Hyman Law

Used where appropriate to reason about decision time and the number of available choices.

Fitts's Law

Used where appropriate to evaluate the accessibility and interaction cost of critical touch targets.

These frameworks will only be incorporated where they directly support a design or research decision.

14. Prototype Scope

The existing internship product scope remains unchanged.

The project will include:

- passenger-facing autonomous vehicle interface;
- in-cabin display;
- companion interaction concepts where required;
- autonomous vehicle status information;
- perception information;
- explainable AI interaction;
- emergency interaction;
- degraded-system states;
- accessibility features;
- design system;
- high-fidelity prototype;
- critical interaction flows.

The autonomy stack itself remains outside the project scope.

---

15. Experimental Prototype

A dedicated experimental prototype will be derived from the main product prototype.

The experimental prototype will focus on controlled scenarios in which passenger understanding of autonomous-system behaviour can be measured.

The experimental prototype must remain visually and functionally consistent with the primary product.

The research prototype is therefore not a separate product.

It is an experimentally controlled version of the internship product.

---

16. Safety and Research Integrity

This project is a research and design prototype.

It does not claim production certification, vehicle safety certification, or compliance with functional-safety standards.

Where relevant, safety engineering and Human Factors principles will inform interaction analysis.

The project will not claim that a prototype has achieved a vehicle-level safety integrity classification without the appropriate engineering and safety processes.

Similarly, physiological measurements, eye tracking, or other specialised measurements will only be reported if they are actually collected using appropriate equipment and methodology.

17. Expected Contributions

The project aims to produce four connected outcomes.

Contribution 1 — Product Design

A complete passenger-facing autonomous vehicle interface suitable for the internship deliverable.

Contribution 2 — Human Factors Design

A theoretically grounded explanation of how Human Factors principles informed important interface decisions.

Contribution 3 — Empirical Evaluation

An experimental evaluation of how explanation design affects passenger understanding, trust, workload, and interaction performance.

Contribution 4 — Research Artifact

A research-paper-style document documenting:

- background;
- related work;
- research gap;
- research questions;
- hypotheses;
- system design;
- methodology;
- experimental results;
- discussion;
- limitations;
- design implications;
- future research.

---

18. Project Success Criteria

The project will be considered successful when it demonstrates:

1. A coherent autonomous vehicle passenger experience.
2. A complete internship-quality product deliverable.
3. A functioning high-fidelity prototype.
4. Clear explanation of the Human Factors rationale behind major design decisions.
5. A reproducible experimental methodology.
6. Empirical evidence supporting or challenging the design hypotheses.
7. Appropriate statistical analysis of collected data.
8. Explicit discussion of limitations.
9. A professional research-paper-style report.
10. A portfolio case study that clearly communicates the relationship between research, design, implementation, and evaluation.

---

19. Project Philosophy

The project should not attempt to appear academically advanced merely by adding terminology, theories, or statistical methods.

Every theoretical concept must serve a concrete purpose.

Every major interface decision should have a rationale.

Every research claim must be supported by evidence.

Every result must be reported honestly, including results that contradict the original hypotheses.

The objective is therefore not to make the project appear like a master's thesis.

The objective is to demonstrate the reasoning, methodology, and discipline expected from someone preparing for advanced Human Factors and Human–Machine Interaction research.
