Day 9 — Methodological Literature Analysis

Objective

Move from literature synthesis to methodological extraction.

The focus was on understanding how previous researchers investigated explanation, trust, passenger behaviour, workload, and attention in autonomous-vehicle contexts.

---

Papers Analysed

1. Du et al. (2019)

Primary contribution:

Explanation timing.

Method:

Within-subject driving-simulator experiment with 32 participants.

Project relevance:

Supports experimental manipulation of pre-action versus post-action explanations.

---

2. Ha et al. (2020)

Primary contribution:

Explanation type × perceived risk.

Method:

Simulator experiment using three explanation types and four risk contexts.

Project relevance:

Demonstrates that explanation effects can depend on situational risk.

---

3. Strauch et al. (2019)

Primary contribution:

Passenger gaze and trust.

Method:

Field and simulator investigations.

Project relevance:

Provides direct passenger-focused evidence and cautions against treating gaze as a direct substitute for trust.

---

4. Hong et al. (2025)

Primary contribution:

Multimodal explanation in fully autonomous passenger contexts.

Project relevance:

Shows that modality effectiveness can depend on passenger engagement in non-driving activities.

---

5. Lee et al. (2024)

Primary contribution:

Perceived explainability and AV trust/acceptance.

Method:

Survey with 399 participants and structural equation modelling.

Project relevance:

Supports explainability as an important construct but does not directly establish real-time interface effects.

---

Major Methodological Learning

Previous studies differ substantially in:

- Participant role
- Automation level
- Experimental environment
- Explanation design
- Measurement
- Scenario risk
- Interaction task

Therefore, findings should not be combined without considering context.

---

Major Design Decision

The project will prioritize a controlled experimental design in which:

- The scenario is controlled.
- The autonomous event is controlled.
- The explanation is manipulated.
- Human outcomes are measured.

---

Important Limitation

The current literature review is based on accessible publication records, abstracts, and available academic metadata for some papers.

Full-text methodological details will only be added when the actual paper is accessible and reviewed.

No participant count, statistical result, questionnaire, or methodological detail will be invented from an abstract.

Day 10 — Experimental Design

Date: 22 September 2026

Objective

Translate the literature-derived research questions and measurement framework into a controlled experimental design.

Completed

- Defined the primary experimental manipulation.
- Compared explanation presence and explanation specificity as candidate manipulations.
- Selected explanation condition as the initial experimental focus.
- Defined three candidate explanation conditions:
  - No explanation
  - Minimal explanation
  - Contextual explanation
- Defined primary dependent variables.
- Defined secondary dependent variables.
- Established principles for controlled autonomous-vehicle scenarios.
- Developed four candidate experimental scenarios.
- Defined preliminary scenario-selection criteria.
- Established the need for randomization and counterbalancing if multiple conditions are presented to the same participant.
- Defined pilot-study requirements.
- Documented experimental-integrity constraints.

Key Learning

A research question can contain several theoretically interesting variables without requiring all of them to be manipulated in one experiment.

Testing explanation presence, specificity, timing, modality, passenger activity, and risk simultaneously could produce an unnecessarily complex design and make causal interpretation difficult.

The current approach therefore prioritizes explanation condition as the principal manipulation while retaining the remaining variables as possible future extensions or controlled scenario characteristics.

Key Methodological Decision

The initial experiment will focus on the following comparison:

«No explanation → Minimal explanation → Contextual explanation»

The autonomous-vehicle behaviour should remain consistent across conditions.

The intended experimental difference is therefore the information presented by the passenger interface.

Primary Outcomes

- Passenger understanding
- Trust
- Cognitive workload

Secondary Outcomes

- Situation awareness
- Interaction performance
- Perceived transparency
- Usability
- Response time, where appropriate

Important Limitation

The experimental design is currently preliminary.

The following remain to be finalized:

- within-subject vs between-subject structure;
- exact number of participants;
- final scenarios;
- participant tasks;
- questionnaire selection;
- randomization;
- counterbalancing;
- sample-size/power analysis;
- statistical analysis plan.

No participant number or statistical result will be invented before these decisions are completed.

Research Integrity Note

The experiment will evaluate a passenger-facing interface under controlled conditions.

It will not be presented as validation of autonomous-driving algorithms, production safety, certification, or real-world autonomous-vehicle performance.
