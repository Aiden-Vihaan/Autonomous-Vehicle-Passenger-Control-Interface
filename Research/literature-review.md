# Literature Review

## Project

**Autonomous Vehicle Passenger Control Interface**

## Purpose

This literature review establishes the academic foundation for the Human Factors and HCI component of the project.

The review focuses on research relevant to passenger interaction with highly automated vehicles, explainable AI, trust in automation, situation awareness, cognitive workload, human-autonomy interaction, and safety-critical interface design.

The purpose is not to collect papers simply for citation. Each source will be evaluated for how it contributes to the research questions, theoretical framework, methodology, or design decisions.

---

# Review Objectives

The literature review will investigate five major areas:

1. Human interaction with highly automated vehicles
2. Explainable AI and human understanding
3. Trust and trust calibration in automation
4. Situation awareness and cognitive workload
5. Safety-critical and accessible interaction design

---

# Literature Review Questions

### LRQ1 — Autonomous Vehicle Interaction

How do passengers understand and interact with highly automated vehicles when they are not directly controlling the vehicle?

### LRQ2 — Explainability

How does explaining automated-system behaviour influence human understanding, transparency, and interaction?

### LRQ3 — Trust

What factors influence trust in automated systems, and how can interface design support appropriate trust calibration?

### LRQ4 — Workload

How does additional information affect cognitive workload during unexpected or safety-relevant situations?

### LRQ5 — Situation Awareness

How can autonomous systems communicate information that helps passengers maintain an appropriate understanding of the current situation?

### LRQ6 — Safety Communication

What Human Factors principles are relevant when communicating unexpected, uncertain, or safety-critical autonomous-system states?

# Inclusion Criteria

A source may be considered relevant when it:

- Addresses human interaction with automated or autonomous systems.
- Investigates explainability or transparency of AI/automation.
- Examines trust or trust calibration in automation.
- Addresses situation awareness or cognitive workload.
- Provides relevant Human Factors or HCI theory.
- Investigates passenger interaction with automated vehicles.
- Provides evidence relevant to safety-critical interface communication.
- Provides an established methodological approach relevant to the planned evaluation.

---

# Exclusion Criteria

Sources may be excluded when they:

- Focus exclusively on autonomous-driving algorithms without a human interaction component.
- Address vehicle engineering without relevance to passenger experience.
- Provide unsupported commercial claims.
- Are primarily promotional rather than evidence-based.
- Do not provide sufficient methodological or conceptual relevance to the research questions.

# Planned Literature Themes

The literature review will be organised into the following themes:

## 1. Human Factors of Highly Automated Vehicles

Understanding how human roles change when vehicle automation increases.

## 2. Explainable AI

Understanding how explanations can communicate system behaviour, reasoning, uncertainty, or intent.

## 3. Trust in Automation

Understanding how system transparency and performance affect trust and reliance.

## 4. Situation Awareness

Understanding how people maintain an appropriate mental model of an automated system and its environment.

## 5. Cognitive Workload

Understanding the relationship between information quantity, complexity, attention, and passenger workload.

## 6. Unexpected and Safety-Relevant Events

Understanding how automated systems should communicate changes in state and situations requiring passenger attention.

## 7. Accessibility and Inclusive Interaction

Understanding how safety-relevant information can be communicated to users with different sensory, cognitive, and motor abilities.

# Research Gap Identification

The project will not assume a research gap before reviewing the literature.

A potential research gap will only be stated after evaluating existing evidence.

The review will specifically examine whether existing research has sufficiently addressed:

- Passenger-facing explanations in highly automated vehicles
- Explanation detail and passenger comprehension
- The relationship between explanation and trust calibration
- Explanation-related cognitive workload
- Interaction performance during unexpected autonomous-vehicle events
- Practical interface patterns for communicating autonomous-system intent

Any final research-gap statement will be supported by the reviewed literature.
