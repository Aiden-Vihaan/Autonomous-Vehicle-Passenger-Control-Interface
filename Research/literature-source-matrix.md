Literature Source Matrix — Day 8 Update

S03 — Ha et al. (2020)

Citation

Ha, T., Kim, S., Seo, D., & Lee, S. (2020). Effects of explanation types and perceived risk on trust in autonomous vehicles. Transportation Research Part F: Traffic Psychology and Behaviour, 73, 271–280.

DOI:

10.1016/j.trf.2020.06.021

Research Area

- Explainable AI
- Trust in automation
- Autonomous vehicles
- Risk

Research Question Relevance

RQ3, RQ5

Method

The study examined three explanation conditions:

- No explanation
- Simple explanation
- Attributional explanation

across autonomous-driving situations with different perceived risk levels.

Key Finding

Explanation type significantly affected trust.

Perceived risk moderated the effect of explanation type on trust.

Interpretation

The effectiveness of an explanation cannot necessarily be separated from the situation in which it is presented.

Project Relevance

This supports treating event risk/urgency as an important contextual factor in our research design.

Design Implication

The interface should not use a single explanation strategy for every event.

Higher-risk situations may require different information presentation from routine events.

Limitation for Our Project

The study concerns autonomous-driving scenarios and trust rather than exactly the same passenger-control interface proposed in this project.

Therefore, its findings should not be directly generalized to all Level 4/5 passenger contexts.

Evidence Strength

Strong relevance for explanation type × risk × trust.

S04 — Du et al. (2019)

Citation

Du, N., Haspiel, J., Zhang, Q., Tilbury, D., Pradhan, A. K., Yang, X. J., & Robert, L. P. Jr. (2019). Look who's talking now: Implications of AV's explanations on driver trust, AV preference, anxiety and mental workload. Transportation Research Part C: Emerging Technologies, 104, 428–442.

DOI:

10.1016/j.trc.2019.05.025

Research Area

- Explanation timing
- Trust
- Mental workload
- Autonomous vehicles

Research Question Relevance

RQ4

Method

Within-subject driving-simulator experiment.

Participants:

32. 

Conditions included:

- No explanation
- Pre-action explanation
- Post-action explanation
- Explanation with approval/disapproval option

Key Finding

Explanations provided before an AV acted were associated with higher trust and preference than explanations provided after the action.

No significant difference was reported for anxiety and workload in the relevant comparison.

Interpretation

Explanation timing may influence how users interpret autonomous behaviour.

Project Relevance

This directly supports investigating explanation timing in the proposed experiment.

Design Implication

Safety-relevant explanations should be considered before the relevant system action when feasible.

Limitation for Our Project

The participants were interacting in a driving context rather than a purely passenger-oriented Level 4/5 robotaxi interface.

Therefore, the finding provides theoretical and empirical support but not direct proof for our specific passenger interface.

Evidence Strength

Strong for explanation timing and trust.

S05 — Hong et al. (2025)

Citation

Hong, J., Kim, S., & Lee, S. (2025). Enhancing passenger-vehicle interaction through multimodal explanation for unexpected behaviors of fully autonomous driving in non-driving-related tasks. Transportation Research Part F: Traffic Psychology and Behaviour, 109, 1350–1364.

Research Area

- Passenger interaction
- Multimodal XAI
- Unexpected autonomous behaviour
- Situation awareness
- Trust
- Non-driving-related tasks

Research Question Relevance

RQ1, RQ5, RQ6

Method

Experiment with:

70 participants.

The study examined:

- Passenger engagement in non-driving-related tasks
- Text explanations
- Speech explanations
- Unexpected driving situations

Eight unexpected situations were included.

Key Finding

Speech explanations increased situation awareness and trust when passengers were not engaged in non-driving-related tasks.

Visual explanations improved pragmatic UX for passengers engaged in non-driving-related tasks.

Multimodal explanations were not universally beneficial.

Interpretation

Explanation effectiveness depends on passenger context.

More modalities do not automatically produce better outcomes.

Project Relevance

This is especially important because our project is explicitly passenger-oriented.

Design Implication

The interface should consider passenger attention and current activity before presenting rich explanations.

Limitation

The study context, scenarios, and interface implementation differ from the proposed prototype.

Evidence Strength

Very strong relevance for passenger-focused explanation research.

S06 — Lee et al. (2024)

Citation

Lee et al. (2024). Critical roles of explainability in shaping perception, trust, and acceptance of autonomous vehicles. International Journal of Industrial Ergonomics, 100, 103568.

DOI:

10.1016/j.ergon.2024.103568

Research Area

- Explainability
- Trust
- AV acceptance

Research Question Relevance

RQ1, RQ2, RQ3

Method

Survey study involving:

399 participants.

Participants were exposed to different AV introductions, including conditions containing how/why explanations.

Key Finding

Perceived explainability had a strong influence on trust and acceptance.

Interpretation

Explainability may be an important psychological factor in AV interaction.

Project Relevance

Provides additional evidence for examining explainability as an independent variable.

Limitation

The study focused primarily on perception, trust, and acceptance rather than the specific real-time passenger interaction behaviour investigated in this project.

Evidence Strength

Strong for explainability → trust/acceptance.

S07 — Strauch et al. (2019)

Citation

Strauch, C., Mühl, K., Patro, K., Grabmaier, C., Reithinger, S., Baumann, M., & Huckauf, A. (2019). Real autonomous driving from a passenger's perspective: Two experimental investigations using gaze behaviour and trust ratings in field and simulator. Transportation Research Part F: Traffic Psychology and Behaviour, 66, 15–28.

DOI:

10.1016/j.trf.2019.08.013

Research Area

- Passenger Human Factors
- Trust
- Gaze behaviour
- Safety-critical situations

Research Question Relevance

RQ1, RQ4, potential future attention analysis

Method

Two experimental investigations:

- Real autonomous vehicle
- Driving simulator

The researchers examined passenger gaze behaviour and trust.

Key Finding

Passengers showed more fixations toward safety-relevant areas during safety-critical situations.

Trust and gaze behaviour were also affected by driving mode and driving style.

Interpretation

Passenger attention changes according to situational relevance.

Project Relevance

Supports the idea that safety-critical information should be designed according to passenger attention and situation.

Limitation

Gaze behaviour is not automatically equivalent to situation awareness or comprehension.

Evidence Strength

Strong for passenger attention in autonomous driving.

Research Area

- Passenger HMI
- Trust
- Transparency
- Riding comfort

Key Finding

Recent on-road research investigated how displaying dynamic path trajectories affected passenger perceptions of transparency, trust, and motion sickness in a commercially operating autonomous vehicle.

Project Relevance

This strengthens the case for treating passenger-facing visualizations as Human Factors variables rather than purely aesthetic UI elements.

Limitation

The study investigates dynamic trajectory visualization and riding experience rather than explanation timing or explanation specificity.

Cross-Source Synthesis

Across the reviewed literature, five patterns are emerging:

1. Explainability can influence trust.
2. Explanation type matters.
3. Explanation timing matters.
4. Risk/context can moderate explanation effects.
5. Passenger activity can change whether explanations are useful.

Therefore:

«The central research problem should not be framed as "Do explanations improve trust?"»

A more scientifically useful question is:

«"Under what conditions do explanations improve passenger understanding and appropriately calibrated trust without imposing unnecessary cognitive demand?"»

This refinement will guide the experimental design.
