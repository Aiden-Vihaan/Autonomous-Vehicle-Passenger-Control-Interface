Research Variables

Purpose

This document operationalizes the major constructs identified during the literature review.

The final experimental variables will be selected based on:

1. Literature support.
2. Research-question relevance.
3. Feasibility within the project timeline.
4. Measurement reliability.
5. Participant burden.
6. Prototype implementation feasibility.

---

Independent Variables

IV1 — Explanation Presence

Levels:

- No explanation
- Explanation provided

Purpose:

Tests whether providing an explanation changes passenger outcomes.

---

IV2 — Explanation Specificity

Candidate levels:

Level 1 — Minimal

«"Slowing for pedestrian."»

Level 2 — Contextual

«"Pedestrian entering the crossing ahead. Slowing to maintain a safe distance."»

Potential future Level 3:

More detailed explanation containing additional contextual information.

Level 3 will only be retained if literature and pilot testing justify its inclusion.

---

IV3 — Explanation Timing

Candidate levels:

- Pre-action
- Post-action

The pre-action condition communicates the reason before the vehicle performs the relevant action.

The post-action condition communicates the reason after the action.

This variable is supported by previous experimental AV research.

---

IV4 — Explanation Modality

Candidate levels:

- Visual
- Auditory
- Multimodal

This variable requires careful experimental control because modality can affect attention and workload.

Recent passenger research indicates that modality effectiveness can depend on passenger activity.

---

Dependent Variables

DV1 — Comprehension

Possible measurement:

Multiple-choice or forced-choice questions following a scenario.

Example:

«Why did the vehicle slow down?»

Possible responses:

A. Traffic congestion
B. Pedestrian entering crossing
C. Navigation recalculation
D. System malfunction

Primary measure:

Percentage of correct responses.

---

DV2 — Situation Awareness

Possible measurement dimensions:

Level 1 — Perception

Did the participant identify what was happening?

Level 2 — Comprehension

Did the participant understand why it was happening?

Level 3 — Projection

Could the participant predict what the vehicle would do next?

Situation awareness will be operationalized carefully rather than treated as a single vague perception measure.

---

DV3 — Trust

Possible measurements:

- Trust questionnaire
- Confidence in system behaviour
- Willingness to rely on the system
- Perceived reliability

Trust will not be interpreted simply as:

«Higher = better.»

Instead, the study will examine whether trust is appropriate relative to the observed system behaviour.

---

DV4 — Cognitive Workload

Candidate measurement:

NASA-TLX.

Relevant dimensions include:

- Mental demand
- Physical demand
- Temporal demand
- Performance
- Effort
- Frustration

NASA-TLX is widely used for subjective workload assessment.

---

DV5 — Interaction Performance

Possible measures:

- Response accuracy
- Response time
- Task completion
- Interaction errors

---

DV6 — Perceived Transparency

Possible measures:

- Understanding of system reasoning
- Clarity of explanation
- Perceived transparency
- Explanation usefulness

---

Potential Moderating Variables

The following variables may influence the relationship between explanation design and outcomes:

- Perceived risk
- Event urgency
- Passenger engagement
- Prior AV experience
- Familiarity with autonomous systems
- Explanation modality

These will not automatically be included in the final experiment.

Their inclusion will depend on literature evidence and feasibility.

---

Candidate Confounds

Potential confounding variables include:

- Scenario difficulty
- Reading ability
- Familiarity with technology
- Prior experience with AVs
- Individual differences in trust
- Interface visual complexity
- Explanation length
- Audio quality
- Response-device familiarity

These variables should be controlled, randomized, measured, or acknowledged as limitations where appropriate.

---

Operationalization Principle

Every construct used in the final study should answer three questions:

1. What exactly does the construct mean?
2. How will it be measured?
3. Why is that measurement appropriate?

A construct will not be included simply because it sounds relevant to Human Factors.
