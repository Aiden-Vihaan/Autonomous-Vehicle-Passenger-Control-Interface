Conceptual Model

Passenger-Facing Explanations for Autonomous Vehicle Behaviour

1. Purpose

This conceptual model translates the theoretical framework into a testable Human Factors research structure.

The model describes how the presentation of explanations for unexpected autonomous-vehicle behaviour may influence passenger outcomes.

The central assumption is not that one explanation type is automatically optimal.

Instead, the model investigates whether differences in explanation specificity are associated with differences in:

- comprehension;
- response time;
- trust;
- cognitive workload;
- perceived understanding;
- task success;
- interaction errors.

3. Independent Variable

Explanation Condition

The principal independent variable is explanation specificity.

Condition A — No Explanation

The autonomous vehicle performs the unexpected action without a passenger-facing causal explanation.

Condition B — Minimal Explanation

The interface provides a concise explanation of the immediate cause.

Example:

«Slowing for pedestrian.»

Condition C — Contextual Explanation

The interface provides additional contextual information describing the event and vehicle response.

Example:

«Pedestrian entering the crossing ahead. Slowing to maintain a safe distance.»

The three conditions are designed to preserve the same underlying event while varying the amount and specificity of passenger-facing explanation.

---

4. Primary Outcome

Comprehension

Comprehension is the primary dependent variable.

The central question is:

«Can the passenger accurately understand why the autonomous vehicle behaved as it did?»

Potential operationalisation includes:

- comprehension accuracy;
- correct identification of the event;
- correct identification of the vehicle response;
- correct interpretation of the immediate implication.

The primary outcome is therefore behavioural/objective rather than solely perceptual.

---

5. Secondary Outcomes

5.1 Response Time

Response time provides an indication of how quickly participants can interpret and respond to the presented situation.

It may be useful when comparing explanations that differ in information density.

---

5.2 Trust

Trust measures the passenger's reported trust in the autonomous system.

Trust is interpreted alongside comprehension.

The project specifically avoids treating increased trust as automatically desirable.

The relevant Human Factors concept is calibrated trust.

---

5.3 Cognitive Workload

Workload represents the perceived mental demand associated with interpreting the vehicle behaviour and interacting with the interface.

NASA-TLX is the planned principal subjective workload measure.

---

5.4 Perceived Understanding

Participants may report how well they believe they understood the vehicle's behaviour.

This variable is intentionally separated from objective comprehension.

The distinction is:

Objective Comprehension
        ≠
Perceived Understanding

Comparing the two may reveal situations in which passengers feel informed without demonstrating equivalent objective understanding.

---

5.5 Task Success

Task success captures whether participants successfully complete the intended interaction.

Examples include:

- identifying why the vehicle slowed;
- locating relevant information;
- selecting the appropriate passenger-interface response.

---

5.6 Interaction Errors

Interaction errors capture incorrect or unsuccessful interface actions.

Examples may include:

- selecting an incorrect control;
- misunderstanding an interface state;
- failing to locate required information.

---

6. Contextual Factors

The model recognises several contextual factors that may influence the relationships between explanation condition and passenger outcomes.

Event Type

Different unexpected events may produce different interpretation requirements.

Explanation Timing

Information presented before, during, or immediately after vehicle behaviour may produce different cognitive effects.

Passenger Activity

Passengers may be:

- actively monitoring the journey;
- reading;
- watching media;
- interacting with another interface function;
- engaged in another non-driving task.

Urgency

The importance and time pressure associated with an event may influence the usefulness of different explanation formats.

Modality

Information may be delivered through:

- visual;
- auditory;
- haptic;
- multimodal interaction.

Accessibility

The usefulness of a modality may vary according to passenger sensory and motor requirements.

---

7. Proposed Relationships

Relationship R1

Explanation condition → Comprehension

Explanation specificity may influence how accurately passengers understand unexpected vehicle behaviour.

---

Relationship R2

Explanation condition → Trust

Explanation presentation may influence passenger trust-related responses.

The model does not predict that increased trust is inherently better.

---

Relationship R3

Explanation condition → Workload

Increasing explanatory information may change perceived cognitive workload.

The direction of this relationship is intentionally treated as an empirical question.

---

Relationship R4

Comprehension ↔️ Trust

Understanding and trust may be associated.

However, the model treats them as distinct constructs.

---

Relationship R5

Comprehension ↔️ Workload

Higher comprehension does not necessarily imply lower workload.

An explanation could improve understanding while requiring additional cognitive processing.

---

Relationship R6

Context → Explanation Effectiveness

Factors such as passenger activity, event type, timing, urgency, and modality may influence the effectiveness of an explanation.

10. Data Interpretation Logic

The conceptual model supports several possible result patterns.

Pattern A

Higher comprehension
+
Acceptable workload
+
Appropriately calibrated trust

This would indicate a potentially useful explanation design pattern.

Pattern B

Higher comprehension
+
Higher workload

This would indicate a possible understanding–workload trade-off.

Pattern C

Higher trust
+
Low comprehension

This would demonstrate why trust should not be evaluated independently.

Pattern D

No meaningful difference between conditions

This would also be an informative result.

A null finding would indicate that increasing explanation specificity did not produce a measurable difference under the tested conditions.

---

11. Conceptual Model and Research Questions

Primary Research Question

«How does the presentation of explanations for unexpected autonomous-vehicle behaviour affect passenger understanding, trust, and cognitive workload?»

Secondary Questions

1. Does explanation presence affect passenger comprehension?
2. Does explanation specificity affect comprehension and trust-related responses?
3. Does explanation timing influence passenger understanding?
4. Does event risk or urgency influence the usefulness of explanations?
5. Does passenger activity influence the usefulness of visual, auditory, or multimodal explanations?

The experimental implementation primarily tests explanation condition A/B/C.

Timing, risk, passenger activity, and multimodal interaction are retained as contextual and future experimental dimensions unless they are explicitly included in the final tested design.

---

12. Conceptual Boundaries

This model does not claim that:

- one explanation condition will necessarily outperform another;
- more information produces better comprehension;
- higher trust represents better interaction;
- lower workload is always preferable;
- multimodal explanations are universally superior;
- findings from a prototype automatically generalise to real autonomous vehicles.

The model defines relationships to be investigated rather than conclusions that have already been established.

---

13. Design Translation

The conceptual model produces the following design architecture:

EVENT
  ↓
CLASSIFY
  ↓
EXPLAIN
  ↓
PASSENGER INTERPRETS
  ↓
PASSENGER ACTS / CONTINUES

The explanation itself follows:

WHAT HAPPENED
      ↓
WHAT THE VEHICLE IS DOING
      ↓
WHAT IT MEANS
      ↓
OPTIONAL DETAIL

This structure provides a direct connection between the research framework and the passenger HMI.

---

14. Final Conceptual Statement

The conceptual model positions passenger-facing explanations as an interaction mechanism between autonomous-vehicle behaviour and human interpretation.

The central research proposition is:

«The design of an explanation is not only a communication problem; it is a Human Factors problem involving comprehension, trust calibration, cognitive workload, situation awareness, accessibility, timing, and safety-oriented interaction.»

This proposition forms the conceptual foundation for the experimental methodology and final design evaluation.
