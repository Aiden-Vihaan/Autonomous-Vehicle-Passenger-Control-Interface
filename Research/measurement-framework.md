Measurement Framework

Purpose

This document connects each research construct to a potential measurement strategy.

The objective is to prevent vague outcomes such as:

«"The interface felt better."»

Each major construct should have an operational measurement.

---

1. Comprehension

Construct

Whether the passenger correctly understands why the autonomous vehicle performed an action.

Candidate Measure

Scenario-based comprehension questions.

Example:

«Why did the vehicle slow down?»

Possible answers:

- Pedestrian entering crossing
- Navigation change
- Traffic congestion
- System malfunction

Primary Metric

Percentage correct.

---

2. Situation Awareness

Situation awareness can be considered at three levels:

Level 1

Perception of relevant information.

Level 2

Understanding the meaning of that information.

Level 3

Projection of what will happen next.

Candidate Measurement

Scenario questions addressing:

- What is happening?
- Why is it happening?
- What will the vehicle do next?

---

3. Trust

Construct

Passenger confidence that the autonomous system will behave appropriately.

Important Principle

Higher trust is not automatically better.

The study should distinguish:

Low trust
Appropriate trust
Overtrust

where possible.

---

4. Cognitive Workload

Candidate Instrument

NASA-TLX.

Potential dimensions:

- Mental demand
- Physical demand
- Temporal demand
- Performance
- Effort
- Frustration

NASA-TLX should only be used in the final study after confirming the exact administration and scoring procedure.

---

5. Interaction Performance

Potential measures:

- Response accuracy
- Response time
- Task completion
- Number of errors

---

6. Perceived Transparency

Candidate measures:

- "I understood why the vehicle behaved this way."
- "The system's reasoning was clear."
- "The explanation was useful."

These items should not be treated as validated scales unless their source and validation are established.

---

7. Eye Tracking

Eye tracking is a possible secondary measure.

Potential measures:

- Fixation duration
- Fixation count
- Time to first fixation
- Fixations on safety-relevant regions

However, Strauch et al. demonstrate why gaze should not automatically be interpreted as trust or awareness.

Therefore:

«Eye tracking is optional rather than a required primary measure.»

---

Measurement Priority

Primary

1. Comprehension
2. Trust
3. Cognitive workload

Secondary

4. Situation awareness
5. Interaction performance
6. Perceived transparency

Optional

7. Eye tracking
8. Physiological measures

Physiological measures will only be included if suitable equipment and ethical procedures are genuinely available.

---

Measurement Principle

The project will avoid collecting measurements merely because they are technologically interesting.

Every measurement must directly support at least one research question or hypothesis.
