Human Factors Interpretation

1. Situation Awareness

Unexpected vehicle behaviour creates an information gap.

Vehicle behaviour
      ↓
Passenger notices deviation
      ↓
"Why?"
      ↓
Interpretation
      ↓
Trust / concern / action

The explanation interface intervenes between unexpected behaviour and passenger interpretation.

---

2. Mental Model Support

The interface does not expose the complete autonomous-driving stack.

Instead it provides a passenger-appropriate mental model:

What happened?
Why?
What is the vehicle doing?
What does this mean for me?

---

3. Cognitive Compatibility

The interface follows passenger-oriented information structures rather than internal engineering architecture.

Situation → reason → action → consequence

---

4. Calibrated Trust

The project adopts:

«Understanding before reassurance.»

The system should not use explanations primarily as persuasive language.

---

5. Accessibility

Accessibility is treated as interaction architecture.

The design supports:

- screen readers,
- logical focus order,
- voice operation,
- large text,
- high contrast,
- captions,
- visual equivalents,
- simplified interaction.

---

6. Final Human Factors Principle

«Explain enough to support understanding, but not so much that the explanation itself becomes the new source of cognitive load.»
